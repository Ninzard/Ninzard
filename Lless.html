Julian Lancaster (born 17 October 2000), known by the handle Lless (a portmanteau indicating "L-less"), is an American polemicist, political essayist, and former Ninzard official who served as the 3rd District General of Scope (2017) and briefly as a Scopian Representative in Legislature XI (2026).1,2,3

Recruited in early 2014 by Nightwork founder Elusion as a teenage intellectual enforcer, Lancaster became known for an aggressive "dirtbag left" debating style combining Marxist-Leninist critique, Baudrillardian semiotics, and unvarnished personal derision.3 After taking over the remnants of Christian Akita's Home Party in 2016, Lancaster rebranded it as the "Homo Party" and won election as District General of Scope in June 2017, serving a single term marked by escalating conflict with Sacraday General Elsy.1,4,5

Following years of intermittent participation, Lancaster re-entered active politics in March 2026, winning election to Legislature XI as an independent.6 During the constitutional debates over the Ninzard Charter, he authored the controversial manifesto The Precession of the Ninzard Simulacra and Archival Fetishism, condemning the Capital's "controlled burn" strategy and calling for the destruction of the historical archives.7 On 18 May 2026, Lancaster sparked community-wide condemnation when he publicly celebrated the suicide of former General Liam Higgins as a beneficial elimination of a fascist, calling the subsequent mourning performative.8,9 He was recalled by a 102–18 vote in Scope on 24 May 2026 and replaced by Sable.10

Early life and recruitment (2014–2016)

Lancaster was born on 17 October 2000 and grew up in Chicago, Illinois. He later enrolled in a prestigious university graduate program in philosophy, which he abandoned after describing modern academia as an "HR-department circlejerk."3

In early 2014, Mark Ionescu (Elusion) encountered a fourteen-year-old Lancaster in an external gaming chatroom dismantling an adult user's argument on Marxist dialectics. Impressed by his command of theory and rhetorical brutality, Elusion recruited him into Nightwork to serve as an intellectual guard dog against aggressive rivals in Sacraday and the emergent Sacranight legion.3 Lancaster remained loyal to Elusion until the latter integrated Nightwork into the Ninzard Project in 2015.3

During the 2016 presidential election, Lancaster supported twelve-year-old Reuben Park out of an accelerationist desire to dismantle the community's establishment, viewing Apollos as an abrasive hammer against the "neoliberal institutions" of the First Triumvirate.3 Following the resignation of President Akita, Lancaster took control of the remnants of the Home Party, quietly rebranding it as the "Homo Party."4

District General of Scope (2017)

In June 2017, Lancaster ran for District General of Nightwork-Scope under the Homo Party banner, defeating incumbent Resistance leader Trixy in a close race.1 His victory was celebrated by Scopian trolls as a rejection of political seriousness.1

Lancaster's generalship was characterized by sharp ideological friction with Sacraday General Michelle Dorio ("Elsy"). Lancaster routinely mocked Dorio's emotional outbursts and public conduct. After Sacraday dissidents leaked photographs and personal information regarding Dorio to Lancaster, he circulated the material across Scope and legislative channels, subjecting Dorio to sustained public ridicule and prompting Dorio to issue direct death threats against him.5

In the December 2017 elections, Lancaster was defeated for re-election by Alaric Adler, an Original Party troll who out-campaigned him by aggressively targeting Lancaster's bisexuality.11 Lancaster's defeat marked the definitive extinction of the Home Party tradition. He declined to contest subsequent general elections, operating as a roving critic on the periphery of Scope.11

Interim eras and tactical voting (2018–2025)

Lancaster regarded the June 2018 "Tone-Deaf Election" and the subsequent RSN War as a "bourgeois circus show," boycotting server events to play Dark Souls III.3 During Syn's tenure as Governor of Nightwork (2020–2022), Lancaster despised the administration's authoritarian lockdown policies. In October 2020, he openly supported Caleb Jensen's populist challenge, though he subsequently voted for Syn in all three 2021 elections purely because he found Syn's opponents even more distasteful.3

In the 2020 presidential election, Lancaster voted for Apollos while complaining that the Capital was monopolizing authority.3 In July 2022, he cast a protest ballot for Al Commando's satirical Poo Party campaign to accelerate the delegitimization of the electoral system.3 During the January 2023 snap election between Catx3 and Hokan, Lancaster refused to participate, describing both figures as existential parasites.3

In September 2025, Lancaster aligned with the Apollos–Spinzle ticket on strictly pragmatic grounds to ensure the defeat of Caleb Jensen, whom he viewed as an unprincipled frat boy pandering to neo-Nazis.3

Legislature XI and ideological polemics (2026)

In the March 2026 snap elections, Lancaster ran for Representative in Scope as an independent, placing first out of four candidates with 83 of 128 votes (64.8%).6 Taking his seat in Legislature XI, Lancaster became the chief intellectual adversary of the Preservate-led Charter initiative, targeting the far-right, the liberal establishment, and the post-left alike.3

Critique of Lyman and esoteric fascism

When Volley Representative Lyman published an essay in April 2026 arguing that the anime series K-On! represented an esoteric blueprint for a Hyperborean ethnostate, Lancaster launched a blistering public deconstruction:12

K-On! is the peak manifestation of late-stage capitalist commodification. What you find spiritual salvation in is engineered by a Japanese mega-corporation designed specifically to extract capital from lonely, alienated men like you by selling them a conflict-free hyperreality. … You dress up your social ineptitude in Julius Evola quotes because admitting that you're just an abrasive, socially isolated retard is too damaging to your fragile ego.

— Lless, responding to Lyman, 4 April 2026

When Lyman replied by comparing Lancaster's dialectics to an accountant auditing a cathedral, Lancaster doubled down, arguing that esoteric traditionalism was an aesthetic retreat from material struggle:12

Marxism, whatever else one thinks of it, does at least stake its claims in the material world: it proposes the reorganization of labor, the restructuring of ownership, the dismantling of systems that produce alienation in the first place. … What you're offering, by contrast, is retreat aestheticized into a philosophy. … You ended your manifesto by preaching the “Aryan duty” to touch grass, lift heavy stone, and participate in the physical world. Yet you wrote this thesis on Azumanga Daioh and esoteric dialectics in a Discord chatroom.

— Lless, 4 April 2026

The Precession of the Ninzard Simulacra

On 11 April 2026, Lancaster published his major theoretical work, The Precession of the Ninzard Simulacra and Archival Fetishism, in which he argued that Ninzard had devolved into a Baudrillardian simulation where the documentation preceded reality:7

In Ninzard, the Archives have preceded reality. Nobody here acts organically. Every feud, every raid, every use of a racial slur, and every time the grown Ninzardian adult achieves the near-mythical feat of stepping outside, is manufactured specifically to generate content and recognition for the historical record. They care about Sign-Value. Because they are alienated, unemployed, and entirely stripped of material purpose in the real world, they substitute Ninzard infamy for actual human legacy.

— Lless, The Precession of the Ninzard Simulacra, 11 April 2026

Lancaster proposed three radical policy mandates: (1) the total liquidation of the biographical Ninzard Archives to strip bad actors of their sign-value; (2) the termination of the Ninzard Career Initiative, which he labeled "the most laughably naive, neoliberal HR-department bullshit ever conceived" that filtered out stable adults and concentrated systemic toxicity; and (3) an immediate policy of open borders designed to bait far-right extremists into real-world felonies so they could be incarcerated by federal law enforcement.7

The essay provoked immediate condemnation across the political spectrum. President Apollos agreed with Lancaster's archival critique but rejected his carceral border plan as "asinine, immoral, and insane."13 Syn denounced Lancaster as a "Marxist accelerationist terrorist," while Waffle Representative Lesty questioned his willingness to endanger marginalized members as bait.13

Clash with Spinzle on the Charter

During the floor debates on the Charter on 8 May 2026, Lancaster attacked the Capital's "controlled burn" doctrine, clashing directly with Vice President Spinzle:14

The hospital metaphor is so perfectly, delightfully neoliberal that it practically writes my response for me. … You are violently evicting the alienated back into the exact same material conditions that drove them to mental illness in the first place, and you have the absolute audacity to pat yourself on the back and call it a cure. … You didn't take the trash out. You just sprayed Febreze over a rotting corpse and called it a successful public health initiative.

— Lless, to Vice President Spinzle, 8 May 2026

When Spinzle countered that even low-wage employment provided material leverage and autonomy, Lancaster derided her as a "1980s Ronald Reagan bootstrapper" whose technocratic utopia merely sanitized the carceral state.14 On 11 May 2026, Lancaster voted "No" on the final Charter draft, stating: “A beautifully formatted neoliberal suicide pact that will do absolutely nothing to solve the material alienation of this server's demographic. I refuse to sign my name to an HR document masquerading as a social contract.”15

Soda suicide controversy and recall (May 2026)

At 02:33 PST on 18 May 2026, former Scope General Liam Higgins (Soda) died by suicide on a live video stream within a Scope voice channel.8 While the community went into shock and leadership opened crisis intake channels, Lancaster posted a message openly mocking the death:8

He did it for the lore. What an epic gamer. He did us all a fucking favor.

— Lless, reacting to the suicide of Liam Higgins, 18 May 2026

When members condemned his reaction, Lancaster escalated his rhetoric across legislative and Scopian channels, arguing that Higgins's passing was the logical outcome of a fascist ideology and condemning the mourning as hypocritical:8,9

A fascist degenerate blows his brains out on a Discord stream, and within hours, you people have already commodified his suicide into a sanctimonious, HR-managed grief parade. … He lived for the simulacrum. He died for the simulacrum. Now you're all gathered around his digital corpse trying to extract the last drops of sign-value from it. … Scope—and every other fucking Ninzard server—is safer today than it was yesterday. Good riddance.

— Lless, in Legislature XI, 18 May 2026

The outburst unified the Scopian electorate against him. District General Hyperio initiated an immediate recall under Section 13 of the Charter, declaring: “You don't speak for Scope. You don't speak for anyone but your own miserable ego. Keep Liam's name out of your fucking mouth.”9 Lancaster sneered at the threat:9

Oh no, not my imaginary title in the digital treehouse! How will I ever recover my material assets? Please, Hyperio, initiate the recall. Spare me the obligation of having to read any more of this bourgeois grief-counseling circlejerk.

— Lless, responding to his recall notice, 18 May 2026

On 24 May 2026, the Scopian electorate voted 102 to 18 to remove Lancaster from Legislature XI.10 He was replaced the following day by Sable.10 Following his removal, Lancaster remained largely silenced within Scope, with members routinely muting him or dismissing his interventions with the derogatory moniker "SouLless."3,9

Political philosophy and style

Lancaster practiced an uncompromising brand of post-left Marxism, drawing heavily from Jean Baudrillard, Slavoj Žižek, and Mark Fisher.3 Unlike progressive leftists like Lesty, Lancaster rejected identity politics, content warnings, and speech policing, viewing them as managerial mechanisms designed to pacify the working class while preserving capitalist relations.3

In debates, Lancaster functioned as an intellectual predator. Rather than trading conventional insults, he specialized in real-time psychological deconstruction, dissecting his opponents' theoretical contradictions and personal neuroses.3 His handle "Lless" reflected his conviction that an individual who refuses to commit to sentimental institutions can never suffer an authentic defeat.3
